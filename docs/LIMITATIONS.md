# MetroPulse Limitations

## Model Limitations

### 1. Spatial Distribution is Synthetic

Cell-level values (economic activity, employment, real estate, congestion) are generated by distance-decay formulas from city center. While multi-center anchors provide some realism, the spatial patterns are **not derived from actual spatial data** (satellite imagery, GPS traces, property registrations).

**Impact**: The map shows plausible patterns but not real spatial distributions. A cell labeled "high economic activity" may not actually have high economic activity in reality.

### 2. Simulation Coefficients are Unvalidated

The core economic model parameters (α=0.5, β=0.35, γ=0.12, etc.) are manually tuned, not from published econometric research or validated against historical outcomes.

**Impact**: The magnitude of simulated effects (e.g., "GDP drops 4.2%") should be interpreted as directional guidance, not quantitative predictions.

### 3. Flood Risk is a Heuristic

Flood risk uses distance-from-center and port-city flags as proxies. It does not incorporate actual hydrology, elevation data, drainage infrastructure, or historical flood records.

**Impact**: Flood risk maps show plausible patterns but should not be used for actual flood risk assessment.

### 4. News Sentiment is a Proxy

Real-time data comes from news article counts and keyword-based sentiment scoring. This is **not** direct measurement of economic activity, employment, or property values.

**Impact**: A negative news sentiment score means more negative articles about a city, not that the city's economy actually declined.

### 5. Cost Proxies are National Averages

The translator uses fixed cost proxies (e.g., ₹450/day for auto fuel, ₹15,000/month for 2BHK rent) applied uniformly to all 12 cities. Actual costs vary significantly between cities.

**Impact**: Rupee-denominated figures are ballpark estimates, not city-specific calculations.

### 6. No Ground-Truth Validation

MetroPulse has not been validated against historical outcomes. There is no evidence that simulated GDP changes, employment effects, or real estate impacts correspond to what actually happens in Indian cities.

### 7. Case Studies are Generalized

The 20 case studies describe real historical events but with generalized outcomes. They do not cite specific quantitative data, peer-reviewed studies, or verified statistics.

### 8. Employment Data is Estimated

India does not publish city-level employment or unemployment data at the granularity MetroPulse requires. All employment figures are estimated from national/state-level data with assumptions about urban vs rural splits.

## Technical Limitations

### 9. Single-Threaded Simulation

The simulation runs on a single thread. For 12 cities with 1400+ H3 cells each, a 24-month simulation takes ~0.5-1.5 seconds. This is acceptable for interactive use but not for batch processing.

### 10. No Real-Time Grid Updates

Real-time data influences the simulation **starting state** but does not update cell values during the simulation loop. The simulation is a one-shot run per scenario, not a continuously updating system.

### 11. Static City Boundaries

City boundaries are circular approximations (14km or 18km radius) unless a GeoJSON file is provided. They do not match actual administrative boundaries.

---

## What Users Should Know

- **This is a model, not reality.** All outputs are estimates from a simplified economic model.
- **Confidence ≠ probability.** A 63% confidence means the model has medium certainty about its inputs, not that there's a 63% chance of the outcome.
- **Use for exploration, not decisions.** MetroPulse is designed to help users understand economic dynamics and test hypotheses, not to make policy decisions.
- **Check the provenance.** Every metric shows whether it's based on real data, estimates, or synthetic formulas. Trust accordingly.
